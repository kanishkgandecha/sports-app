An over is six legal deliveries bowled in a row by the same bowler. Once
an over finishes, a different bowler takes over from the opposite end of
the pitch — the same bowler can't bowl two overs in a row. The over count
you see next to the score (like "14.3 overs") means 14 complete overs
plus 3 balls of the 15th. It's the basic unit of pacing in cricket: a
T20 innings is 20 overs, an ODI innings is 50.
